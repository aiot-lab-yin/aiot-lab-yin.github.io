## Joining the Inaugural ACM Conference on Digital Transformation

We are pleased to announce that our paper, **"Toward Practical Venous Industry Digital Transformation: Lessons from a Long-term Deployment of City-scale Municipal Waste Sensing,"** has been accepted at **ACM DXConf 2026—the inaugural ACM Conference on Digital Transformation**—and is forthcoming.

Authored by **Yin Chen, Wenhao Huang, and Jin Nakazawa**, the paper shares lessons from a long-term municipal waste sensing deployment. We are excited to contribute to the **very first edition of this ACM conference** with findings grounded in the everyday operation of urban waste collection services.

## From Collection Vehicles to City-scale Sensing

Municipal waste management is a representative part of the *Venous Industry*: the services that handle waste and resource recovery after consumption. Digital transformation in this sector is challenging because waste generation and collection take place across many locations, with limited visibility into what happens on the ground.

Our study examines a sensing infrastructure deployed across **three Japanese municipalities and 40 waste collection vehicles**. By using routine collection operations as opportunities to gather data, the deployment demonstrated that **fine-grained municipal waste observability is technically achievable**.

## Why Sensing Alone Is Not Enough

The central lesson is that making waste collection observable is only one step toward practical digital transformation. Once sensing becomes feasible, the main challenges shift toward making the infrastructure sustainable and useful within everyday services.

The paper identifies four areas that future Venous Industry infrastructure must address:

- **Economic scalability:** making deployment and continued operation viable at larger scales.
- **Privacy-aware entity identification:** supporting useful identification while respecting privacy.
- **Citizen-in-the-loop participation:** incorporating citizens into the transformation process.
- **Multi-stakeholder workflow integration:** connecting sensing infrastructure with the work of the different stakeholders involved in municipal waste management.

Drawing on these deployment experiences, the paper derives **four design requirements for future Venous Industry infrastructure**. These findings connect the technical feasibility of city-scale sensing with the economic, privacy, participation, and organizational challenges of practical DX.
